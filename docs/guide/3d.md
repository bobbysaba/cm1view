# 3D

The **3D** workspace draws a box of the run as an interactive 3D scene: isosurfaces of
up to four fields, arrows at a height, streamlines through the flow, and a coloured
floor. It runs in the browser (WebGL), so it needs a browser with WebGL, but the
isosurfaces and streamlines are computed on the server.

## Choosing the region

The 3D scene shows a rectangular box of the domain, from the ground to the *Top*
height. Pick the box in one of three ways:

- The **3D box** tool on the map: drag a box. The app switches to the 3D workspace and
  renders it.
- **Pick region on map** in the 3D toolbar: takes you to the map with the tool on.
- **Use map view**: takes the area now in view on the map.

Press **Render 3D** to draw (again). The toolbar says how big the region is and the
resolution that will be used (`Region 28.4 × 31.0 km · auto res 1/2`). Keep the region
storm-scale (about 50 km across or less) for full resolution. The box also becomes a
region in the Series and Distribution tools ([Series and
distributions](series.md)).

Once a scene is shown, changing a control re-renders it, keeping your view. By default,
moving the time slider re-renders it too (*Re-render when time changes*, in *Scene*).
The scene reads the fields it needs at the shown time, so the first render at a time
loads 3D fields (the status shows progress) and later ones are quicker
([Performance](index.md#performance)). The status line gives the triangle count and
resolution, and notes any isosurface with no surface in the region or that had to be
coarsened because its mesh was too large.

## Isosurfaces

*Isosurfaces* holds four rows, each with a checkbox, a field, a **value**, a colour and
an opacity (0.05 to 1). The defaults are the surfaces most often wanted, and a row is
off if the run lacks its field:

| Row | Default |
|---|---|
| 1 | reflectivity (`dbz`) = 30 dBZ, light blue, opacity 0.22 (on) |
| 2 | w = 10 m/s, orange, opacity 0.85 (on) |
| 3 | w = −5 m/s, blue, opacity 0.7 (off) |
| 4 | ζ = 0.05 1/s, red, opacity 0.95 (off) |

Any 3D field can be used, derived ones included. The pill shows how many are on.
A storm-relative field (streamwise or crosswise vorticity) names the storm motion it used
in the surface's legend entry.

## Vectors

*Vectors* draws arrows in a horizontal plane at a chosen height, up to three layers, with
the same vectors as [the map](map.md#vectors) (wind, horizontal vorticity, baroclinic
generation). A row has the *Vector*, *Height (km AGL)* (0 is the lowest level), *Frame*
(wind only), *Arrows across region* (6 to 40, default 16), *Ref. arrow* and a colour.
Storm-relative wind uses the storm motion chosen in the Sounding panel. The arrows are
lifted slightly above the floor when the height is near zero so the floor does not hide
them; the legend keeps the true height.

## Streamlines

*Streamlines* (tick its checkbox to show them) are instantaneous: traced through the
(u, v, w) of the shown time, forward, backward or both ways, from an n × n grid of seeds
at a chosen height.

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Seed height (km AGL)** | The height of the seed plane (default 0.5). |
| **Seeds across (n × n)** | 3 to 30, default 12 (so 144 lines). |
| **Max length (km)** | 5 to 150, default 40. With *Both*, half of it each way. |
| **Direction** | *Forward* (default), *Backward* or *Both*. |
| **Frame** | *Ground-*, *Grid-* or *Storm-relative* (default). Storm-relative uses the storm motion selected in the sounding panel. |
| **Color by** | *Height* or *Vertical velocity*. |
| **Seed box on map** | Drag a box inside the 3D region on the map: the seeds then go there, n × n of them, and the lines still run until they leave the region. The box is outlined on the map and, dashed at the seed height, in the scene. **Whole region** goes back to seeding everywhere. |

A line ends

- on the region's edge (or the scene's top),
- at *Max length*, or
- where the flow stalls (under 0.3 m/s, or turning back on itself at a stagnation
  point).

A line that reaches the lowest model level slides along it. The lines are traced again
only when something they depend on changes (time, region, top, seed settings, frame),
so changing isosurfaces, the floor or the camera does not re-trace them, and keeps the
selection and pins described next.

### Selecting and comparing streamlines

- **Click a streamline** to select it: it turns yellow, its seed is marked, and the other
  streamlines turn grey and faint until nothing is selected or pinned. A card at the
  lower right plots a field along it against distance from the seed (negative is
  upstream), with the line's height on the right axis, dashed. The field can be any 3D
  field, the speed, or the streamwise vorticity in the streamline's frame. The axes fit
  the field; scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and use the card's toolbar for box zoom or
  reset. **⤢** enlarges the card.
- **Moving the pointer** along the card's plot puts a dot at that distance on each plotted
  line in the scene, in the line's colour.
- The selection follows the same seed (position and height) when the scene re-renders,
  for example when time changes.
- **Pin** (the pin button on the card) keeps the line in a colour of its own (in the
  scene, with its seed, and on the card, its height dashed), and the next click selects
  another. Pin as many as you like: the card plots them all, each against distance from
  its own seed, and lists them as #1, #2, …. Click a pinned line to select it again, and
  press the pin to unpin it. Pins follow their seeds through re-renders; a pin whose seed
  is not in the new scene is counted as "not in this scene" and comes back when it is.
- **×** clears the selection and every pin.
- Isosurfaces in front of a line can catch the click: lower their opacity or switch them
  off.

## Floor

The *Floor* pill (with its checkbox) colours the ground of the region by a field: a 2D
field, or a 3D field at one height (*Height*, where 0 is the lowest level). It starts as
composite reflectivity when the run has it. You can choose the colormap (default: the
field's own), *Reverse*, and the colour *Range*: *From the data* (1st to 99th
percentile, symmetric about 0 for signed fields), *Field default*, or *Custom* with
*Min* and *Max*.

## Scene

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **View from** | *Southwest (default)*, *Above*, *South*, *North*, *West* or *East*. Jumps to the viewpoint without recomputing. |
| **Mouse drag** | What a left-drag does: *Rotate* (turntable, default), *Free rotate*, *Move (pan)* or *Zoom*. |
| **Top (km)** | How high the scene goes (2 km up to the model top, default 15). |
| **Vertical exaggeration** | 1 to 6, default 2. |
| **Smoothing σ** | A Gaussian smoothing, in grid points of the (thinned) scene grid (0 to 3, default 0.75), applied to the field before the isosurface is found. It removes grid-scale noise; 0 turns it off. |
| **Resolution** | *Auto* (about 12 million voxels, thinned to every nth grid point), *Full*, 1/2, 1/3 or 1/4. |
| **Re-render when time changes** | On by default. |

In the scene: drag to rotate, right-drag to move (or set *Mouse drag* to *Move*), scroll
to zoom, double-click to reset. The legend names each surface with its value and units.
The plotly toolbar offers more, including saving a PNG.

## Items tray

The 3D box appears in the items tray under the map; its cube icon opens it in 3D again
and ✕ removes it.
